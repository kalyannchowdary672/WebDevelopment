Deep in the ocean where sunlight cannot reach, many creatures make their own light using a fascinating chemical process called bioluminescence. This glow-in-the-dark ability is created when a substance called luciferin mixes with oxygen, a reaction sparked by an enzyme inside the animal's body. From shimmering jellyfish to eerie deep-sea anglerfish, marine life uses these glowing lights to communicate, camouflage themselves from predators, or lure in their next meal. Even on land, this magical phenomenon can be seen in the familiar summer twinkle of fireflies and the faint glow of certain forest mushrooms. It is one of nature’s most beautiful and practical survival tools.
